# The Steel Colossus Roadmap

The Colossus is four tracks built together: **the Suit**, **ADAM** (the AI), **the Operation** (workshop to company) and **the Mech** (piloted machines). Each track feeds the others. ADAM runs the suit, the suit funds the operation, and the operation builds the mech.

Each stage pays for the next one. Nothing gets bought until the stage before it has earned its share.

Costs are rough 2026 estimates for a solo builder. Check them before committing to a stage.

## Track S: The Suit

| Stage | What you build | Rough cost | Skill it teaches | What it lets you sell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **S1. Gauntlet** ([plan](stage-one.md)) | Printed gauntlet with a gesture- and voice-fired palm repulsor | $380–430 | 3D printing, microcontrollers | Gauntlet STLs, printed parts, the build video series |
| **S2. Helmet** | Motorized faceplate, LED eyes, an inside camera and display feeding ADAM, with fiberglass or carbon fiber reinforcement | $600–1,200 | CAD, servos, composites, embedded vision | Helmet STLs and kits, commissions, the first sponsorship pitch |
| **S3. Chest and arc light** | Chest plate with a glowing arc reactor and a battery pack that powers the gauntlet and helmet | $800–1,500 | Power systems, battery safety, custom PCBs | Arc reactor kits, PCB designs (PCBWay or JLCPCB sponsorship) |
| **S4. Full armor** | Complete wearable suit with metal plates cut by a service like SendCutSend over a printed and composite base | $5,000–15,000 | Metalwork, fit and articulation, systems integration | Convention appearances, paid commissions, a documentary series |
| **S5. Strength assist** | Powered exoskeleton joints on the legs and arms to carry the suit's weight | $10,000–50,000 | Actuators, control loops, safety engineering | Engineering consulting, open hardware, grants |
| **S6. Flight** | Jet or electric thrust | $100,000+ | Propulsion, aviation law | — |

## Track A: ADAM

ADAM costs almost nothing in parts, since it's mostly code. Its price is time, and it becomes sellable software early.

| Stage | What ADAM can do | Rough cost | Skill it teaches | What it lets you sell |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A1. Voice** | Wake word, offline speech, spoken replies, fires the gauntlet | $0–30 (USB mic) | Python, speech recognition | Open-source repo with sponsors, tutorials |
| **A2. Memory and reasoning** | Remembers you and the build, answers questions with an LLM, applies the justice rules to its decisions | $0–20/mo in API costs | LLM integration, databases | Custom assistant builds for other makers |
| **A3. Workshop control** | Runs the printer queue, tracks parts and money in the ledger, watches the workshop through cameras | $100–300 | Home automation, computer vision | Workshop automation kits and setup gigs |
| **A4. Suit HUD** | Live display in the helmet showing suit status, vitals and targets, with full voice control of the suit | $200–600 | Embedded displays, real-time systems | HUD module as a product |
| **A5. JARVIS** | Runs home, workshop, suit and mech as one system, plans builds and orders parts with your approval | Time | Systems architecture | ADAM as a licensed platform |

## Track O: The Operation

| Stage | What it is | Rough cost | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **O1. Bedroom shop** | Laptop, one printer, Etsy and Cults3D listings, a YouTube channel | Covered by S1 | First income, first audience |
| **O2. Registered business** | An LLC, a business bank account and separate books | $100–500 depending on your state | Sponsorship deals, wholesale accounts, tax write-offs on parts |
| **O3. Print farm** | 4 to 10 printers running sales orders while you build | $1,000–3,000, paid from O1 sales | Income that doesn't depend on your hours |
| **O4. Workshop space** | A garage or rented unit with a laser cutter, CNC router and welder | $500–2,000/mo rent plus $5,000–20,000 in tools | In-house metal and composite work for S4 and M1 |
| **O5. Small team** | First contractor or hire for printing, fulfilment or video | Paid from revenue | Frees you to build full time |
| **O6. Manufacturing line** | Kits, HUD modules and exoskeleton parts built to order | Revenue and grant funded | The Colossus becomes the company that builds Colossi |

## Track M: The Mech

| Stage | What you build | Rough cost | Skill it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **M1. Desk mech** | A 1–2 ft walking robot controlled by ADAM | $200–600 | Legged locomotion, inverse kinematics |
| **M2. Rideable rover** | A wheeled or tracked platform you sit on, built on e-bike or mobility-scooter motors | $2,000–6,000 | High-power motors, steel frames, rider safety |
| **M3. Walking exo-mech** | A 10–12 ft piloted frame that walks slowly, building on S5's actuators | $50,000–250,000 | Hydraulics, structural engineering |
| **M4. The Colossus** | Full-scale piloted mech | $1M+ | Everything above, plus a team |

Real piloted mechs exist, such as Japan's Kuratas and Korea's Method-2. They took companies and millions to build, and they barely walk. M3 and M4 come after the Operation reaches O5 or O6, not before.

## The rules

1. **Earn before you buy.** A stage starts when the last one's sales and sponsors cover at least half its cost.
2. **Film everything.** The audience is the funding engine from the first stage onward.
3. **Each stage must work on its own.** A finished gauntlet beats an unfinished suit.
4. **No gambling, loans or credit cards** to speed things up. Stalling a stage is recoverable, but debt isn't.
5. **Safety scales with size.** Anything you wear, ride or pilot gets tested empty before a person is inside it.

## On flight and the full Colossus

Real jet suits exist, such as Gravity Industries', but they cost hundreds of thousands, take years of training, and fall under FAA rules. A full mech is bigger still. These are the stages the rest of the roadmap pays for, not ones to rush. By the time you reach them, you'll have the skills, the money and the team to decide what the Colossus should actually be.
